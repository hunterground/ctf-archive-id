# Licensing and attribution policy

## No blanket content license

Challenge materials are created by many different authors and organizations. The repository's own documentation, metadata, and tooling may have a project license in the future, but that license does not automatically apply to archived challenge materials.

No root-level license should be interpreted as licensing all challenge content.

## Per-challenge assessment

For each challenge, record:

- `declared_license`: the license identifier or exact source wording, if known;
- `redistribution`: `permitted`, `prohibited`, or `unknown`;
- `attribution_required`: whether attribution is required or explicitly requested, where known;
- `attribution`: the attribution text or details, where available;
- `copyright_holder`: the named rights holder, when the source states one;
- `permission_reference`: the license file, notice, or message that supports `redistribution`, when known.

A missing license or unclear notice means `unknown`. Public access to a GitHub repository, website, or downloadable file is not itself permission to redistribute.

## Evidence

Use the narrowest applicable evidence:

1. A license file clearly covering the challenge;
2. A license or notice in the challenge directory;
3. Explicit permission from the author or rights holder;
4. Clear competition terms that address redistribution of challenge materials.

A repository-wide license may not cover third-party challenge content included in that repository. Do not assume it does.

## When redistribution is unknown

Do not copy the challenge files into this repository. Record the source in `archives/repositories.yaml` where useful and document the unresolved status. Ask for permission or further evidence.

## Attribution

Preserve author names, team names, original challenge titles, and source links where known. Do not infer authorship from a Git commit author without corroborating evidence.

## Takedown and correction requests

Treat credible rights-holder requests seriously. Flag the affected material for human review and do not independently make legal determinations. Keep a record of what was changed or removed and why.
